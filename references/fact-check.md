# Fact-check before you search

The single most expensive mistake in this workflow is looking for a frame that
does not exist. Secondary summaries of films are often wrong in a very specific
way: they compress a scene into a memorable line that was never spoken on screen.

## Mandatory when the request names

- a line of dialogue,
- a specific scene ("the confession in episode 3"),
- a speaker,
- an iconic image ("the shot of X doing Y"),
- or a film/year/episode.

## Checklist

1. Title and year — including the correct instalment (sequels and remakes are
   routinely confused).
2. Episode / scene / approximate timestamp, if it is a series.
3. The **original** wording of the line, and the translated wording separately.
4. Who says it, to whom, and in which scene.
5. Whether the widely circulated description is a paraphrase rather than a
   transcript.
6. Whether an "iconic still" is actually promotional or re-staged material.

Use 2-3 independent searches. Prefer transcripts, subtitle files, reviews that
describe the scene, and primary material over listicles. Record the conclusion
in the run notes before searching for images.

## Case 1: the line that was never spoken

A request asked for the frame in *Tsuki ga Kirei* (2017) where the boy confesses
by saying "今夜は月が綺麗ですね" ("the moon is beautiful tonight").

Reality:

- That phrase is the well-known Natsume Soseki anecdote about translating
  "I love you", and the source of the series title. Characters discuss it, but
  it is not the confession line.
- The actual confession is in episode 3: at a shrine at night he starts with
  "つき" (tsuki, "moon"); she looks up thinking he means the moon; he completes
  it as "付き合って" (tsukiatte, "go out with me"). Subtitles read
  "跟我…交往吧…".
- The final episode's train-platform shout is a different line entirely.
- Many Chinese articles compress this into "he confessed by saying the moon is
  beautiful" — a summary, not a frame.

**Resolution:** deliver the real episode-3 frame with its native subtitle, plus
a clearly labelled variant with the requested phrase composited. State in the
delivery that the requested frame does not exist.

## Case 2: the "still" that is not a still

A request asked for the famous shot of the boy's **back of the head** from
*Yi Yi* (2000), widely used as the Criterion Collection cover.

Reality: the cover is a **re-staged photograph** — a look-alike child model shot
by a photographer for the DVD/Blu-ray packaging design, not a frame from the
film. The film does contain the boy photographing people's backs of heads and
the line "you can't see it yourself, so I took it for you", but the famous
cover image is not one of those frames.

**Resolution:** do not deliver the cover as a film still. Use an actual frame of
the boy holding the camera; keep the cover photo only as a reference, labelled.

## Red flags in a request

- A line that is "too quotable" and appears verbatim in many listicles.
- An image described as "iconic" with no scene context.
- A quote attributed across different instalments of a franchise.
- A Chinese phrasing of a Japanese/English line without an original.
- A scene that "everyone knows" but nobody can name the episode for.

When a fact-check fails, say so early and offer the honest alternative (real
adjacent scene, or a clearly labelled composite). Do not silently substitute.
